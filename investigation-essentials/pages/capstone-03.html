Debrief: How FIND Handled It

Why the facts, not the first impression, led to a fair outcome

At first glance, the Missing Temperature Check looks like an open-and-shut case of falsifying a food safety record. Applying FIND told a fuller story — and led to a fairer, more effective outcome.

StageWhat FIND surfaced
F — Frame"Was the required check completed and documented, and if not, what were the circumstances?" — neutral and answerable, not "who falsified the form?"
I — InvestigateMonitoring records, the line-jam log, the training record, and the employee’s account — gathered before forming a view.
N — NarrowEstablished: the check was not done and the form was signed anyway. Contributing causes: the employee was pulled to a line issue, with no coverage process and no training on one. Intent to deceive was not established.
D — Document & DecideAddress the food safety risk, fix the process and retrain, and hold the signing to account through coaching and/or disciplinary action — all documented.
The Point of the Exercise

The same scenario could have supported a different conclusion — if the employee had been trained on the proper protocol for this situation and still signed off, intent might have been established. The conclusion follows the evidence. That is exactly why supervisors should not jump to conclusions, even when a food safety concern looks straightforward.
