Module 4 — N: Narrow Down What Happened

Assessing the Information

Turn a pile of evidence into a clear picture

Once you have gathered the facts, you narrow down what actually happened. This is where you compare what you have, resolve conflicts where you can, and separate what is established from what is merely assumed.

Compare the evidence

Line up the accounts and the records side by side. Do they agree? Where the records and a statement conflict, the documented record is usually the stronger evidence.

Identify gaps and inconsistencies

Note what is missing and where accounts don't line up. A gap isn't proof of anything — it just tells you what you still can't conclude.

Corroborate

Look for two or more independent sources pointing the same way. Corroborated facts carry more weight than a single uncorroborated account.

Separate facts from opinions

"The check wasn't signed" is a fact. "He's lazy" is an opinion. Only facts belong in your findings.

Tip
Watch for your own assumptions. It is easy to favour the evidence that fits your first impression. Weigh every piece on its merits — including the pieces that don't fit your theory.
