Module 1: Understanding Your Role

Investigate, Coach, or Escalate?

Not every concern needs a formal investigation

Part of your role is deciding how to respond to a concern. Some issues are best handled with a quick coaching conversation. Others need fact-finding. And some must go to HR right away. Knowing the difference keeps your response fair and proportionate.

Coach

Use coaching when the facts are clear and the issue is minor — a one-off mistake, a small performance slip, or a simple misunderstanding. No dispute about what happened, no policy breach of consequence. A direct, supportive conversation resolves it.

Investigate

Investigate when the facts are unclear or disputed, when a policy or procedure may have been breached, or when the outcome could lead to discipline. If you need to gather evidence or hear more than one account before you can decide, it is an investigation.

Escalate to HR

Escalate when the matter is serious or sensitive — allegations of harassment, discrimination, violence or threats, potential criminal conduct, or anything involving senior personnel. When in doubt about severity, check with HR before acting.

These concerns fall under Maple Leaf Foods' Respect in the Workplace Policy. Under that policy, a supervisor who becomes aware of harassment or violence must make sure it is addressed — and report it to their Manager, Human Resources, or the Joint Health & Safety Committee — even if no formal complaint has been filed.

Note
These are not always either/or. An investigation may end in coaching. A coaching conversation may surface something that needs HR. Start with the response the facts justify, and adjust as you learn more.
Tip
When unsure, don't guess. If you are not sure whether something needs investigation or should go to HR, ask. Getting the response right early protects everyone involved — including you.
Note
Know your policies: For conduct concerns involving harassment, violence, or disrespect, the Respect in the Workplace Policy is your reference for what to report and to whom. FIND still applies — it is how you establish the facts.
