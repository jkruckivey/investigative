Module 5 — D: Document and Decide

Making Fair Decisions

Let the evidence and the root cause guide the outcome

With the facts established and documented, you decide on the appropriate next step. A fair decision is one that follows from the evidence, accounts for the root cause, and would be the same for anyone else in the same situation.

Base conclusions on evidence

Your decision should rest on the facts you established — not on impressions, history, or how you feel about the person.

Consider the root cause

Use the root cause to target the corrective action — retraining for a training gap, a clearer procedure for a process gap, a systemic fix for a systemic issue. Accountability for a policy breach is applied consistently either way; the root cause shapes what you fix, not whether standards are upheld.

Apply standards consistently

Handle similar situations the same way, regardless of who is involved. Consistency is what makes a decision defensible.

Determine appropriate next steps

This might be coaching, retraining, a process change, a documented conversation, disciplinary action, or escalation to HR — whatever the facts and root cause support. Identifying a root cause does not rule out discipline; where the conduct warrants it, disciplinary action may still be required.

ⓘ
Fix the root cause and apply standards consistently. A finding often calls for both — fixing the system that allowed the error, and applying accountability consistently where a policy was breached. Root-cause thinking shapes the corrective action; it does not change whether standards are enforced.
