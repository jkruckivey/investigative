Module 5 — D: Document and Decide

Documenting Findings

If it isn't documented, it didn't happen

Documentation turns your fact-finding into a clear, defensible record. Good documentation is factual, concise, and organized so anyone reviewing it can follow the line from concern to conclusion. Use a simple, consistent format every time.

A Simple Documentation Format

SectionWhat goes here
IssueThe framed question — the concern you set out to answer.
Evidence ReviewedThe records and accounts you looked at (what, and from where).
Facts EstablishedWhat the evidence shows — facts only, no opinion.
FindingsYour conclusion on the framed question, supported by the facts.
Action RequiredThe appropriate next step, consistent with the findings and root cause.
Key Takeaway
If it isn't documented, it didn't happen. A conversation you didn't record can't support a decision later. Write it down while it's fresh.
Tip
Write it to be read by someone else. Use neutral, factual language. Avoid characterizations like "obviously" or "clearly," and never use the word "guilty" — your job is to establish what happened, not to assign legal guilt.
